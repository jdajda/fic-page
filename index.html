Who we are

A federation born in the canyons

The history of the Fédération Internationale de Canyonisme begins in 2003, with the desire to create an international canyon festival. These events — the Rassemblement Internationale de Canyon (RIC) — still bring canyoneers together every year.

In 2014, French canyoneers joined forces with enthusiasts from around the world to found the International Association of Amateur Canyoning (IAAC), dedicated to recreational canyoning. The word "amateur" travelled poorly between languages, so the association became FIC — in French, "Fédération Internationale de Canyonisme", and in English, the "Federation for International Canyoning".

Our mission

Seven goals, one canyon community

Everything FIC does serves one simple idea: recreational canyoning, done safely, together and in respect of the places we love.

🤝

Network canyoneers

Facilitate networking of canyoneers around the world to exchange information about canyoning.

🌍

Develop the sport

Develop international canyoning as an accessible, recreational discipline in every country.

⚖️

Support associations

Support canyon associations with their political, legal, administrative and economic institutions.

🌿

Respect for nature

Develop respect for nature and encourage low-impact practice inside and around the canyon.

🔬

Share knowledge

Develop scientific knowledge of the practice environment and share it with the community.

🪢

Technique & rescue

Build a common reflection on canyon techniques, trainings and rescue standards.

Board of Directors

The people behind the federation

The board is elected by the member associations and steers the FIC between RICs. Four officers form the executive board; committees and assistants carry the day-to-day work of safety, environment, legal, events and communications.

Canyon Rating

How FIC rates a canyon

One code, three numbers — a vertical difficulty, an aquatic difficulty and a level of commitment, each judged on its own open scale.

v 2 Vertical difficulty
1–7
·
a 3 Aquatic difficulty
1–7
·
C III Commitment & scope
I–VI

A canyon rated v2 a3 III is written 2.3.III — easy on the ropes, moderate in the water, and a half-day commitment. Each column is independent: only one criterion per column sets the category.

Rassemblement Internationale de Canyon

The gathering that started it all

RIC stands for three French words — Rassemblement Internationale de Canyon. The idea came from two people: Rich Carlson from the USA and Koen Viaene from Belgium, who wanted a venue where canyoneers from around the world could meet and share information. Koen organised the first two RICs in Europe, Rich brought them to North America in Mexico.

Since then, local canyoneers have kept co-sponsoring canyon rendezvous around the planet, year after year. Wishes are made, people get excited, plans develop — and a RIC is born. Typically about 200 individuals from at least 15 countries attend.
