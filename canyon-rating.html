Canyon Rating System

One code, three numbers. The FIC rating describes how hard a canyon is to descend vertically, how hard it is in the water, and how far you are committing yourself — independently of each other.

Open scales v1–v7 a1–a7 I–VI

Notation

How a rating is written

v 2 Vertical difficulty
1–7
·
a 3 Aquatic difficulty
1–7
·
C III Commitment & scope
I–VI

Canyons are rated as follows: the letter v followed by one Arabic number from 1 to 7 for the difficulty in the vertical character; the letter a followed by one Arabic number from 1 to 7 for the difficulty in the watery character; and one Roman numeral from I to VI for commitment and scope.

Canyons rated v2 a3 III, v5 a6 V or v4 a5 III. For simplification we write 2.3.III, 5.6.V or 4.5.III.

ℹ️

How to read a rating

Only one of the criteria in each column determines membership in a difficulty category. On a route, avoiding an obstacle or using a specific technique — for example guided abseiling — may result in a lower rating. Jumps are generally considered optional.

Calibration

What the rating assumes

A rating only means something if you know the conditions it was written for.

💧

Average flow

The quotation is valid for an average or ordinary flow, in the usual period of practice — thus at a relatively low level, without necessarily being at low water level.

👥

A group of five

It is calibrated for a group of five people, in a situation of discovery of the canyon, whose level of practice is in adequacy with the technical level of the canyon.

🧭

Habitual, reasoned practice

It is intended for habitual and reasoned practice, with a view to safety and efficiency of movement. A personal search for an increase in difficulty will not add anything to the initial rating.

V — Vertical character

v1 Very easy

  • No vertical obstacle required
  • No recall and no handrail
  • Rope and harness not necessary for normal safe progression
  • No climbing / unclimbing passages

v2 Easy

  • Easy abseiling ≤ 10 m
  • Handrail
  • Easy climbing / unclimbing passage with little exposure

v3 Little difficult

  • Low-flow verticals
  • Reception in a basin with swimming in calm water
  • Simple abseiling, less than 30 m
  • A series of rappels separated by landings allowing the grouping of people
  • Easy handrail
  • Technical walk — slippery, obstacle
  • Climbing / unclimbing passages up to 3c, not very exposed, may require rope

v4 Somewhat difficult

  • Low to medium flow verticals that can begin to cause imbalance or blockage
  • Recall of difficult access and/or greater than 30 m
  • Series of abseils on the wall with comfortable belays
  • Rope friction
  • Difficult handrail
  • Non-visible reminder of the start
  • Reception in a basin with water movement
  • Climbing / descending passages up to 4c or A0, exposed and/or requiring belay

v5 Difficult

  • Medium to high flow rate verticals
  • Difficult recall requiring trajectory and/or balance management
  • A series of abseils on the wall with aerial relays
  • Crossing a suspended overhanging basin during the descent
  • Slippery surface or presence of an obstacle
  • Difficult to remove the rope
  • Exposed climbing / descending passages, up to 5c or A1

v6 Very difficult

  • High to very high flow rate verticals
  • Very difficult recall, requiring effective management of trajectory and/or balance
  • Difficult relay installation — delicate natural mooring, e.g. jammed block
  • Difficult access to the start of the abseil
  • Exposed climbing / descending passage, up to 6a or A2
  • Very slippery or unstable surface
  • Highly emulsified inlet bowl

v7 Extremely difficult

  • Verticals of very strong to extremely strong flow
  • Extremely difficult crossing requiring anticipation and specific management of rope, trajectory, balance, support and rhythm
  • Exposed climbing / descending passages, above 6a or A2
  • Limited visibility and frequent obstacles
  • Passage through a basin with powerful water movements
  • Breathing control — passage in apnea

A — Aquatic character

a1 Very easy

  • No water or walking in calm water
  • Swimming optional
  • No jumping
  • No slide

a2 Easy

  • Swimming of 10 m maximum in calm water
  • Easy jump less than 3 m
  • Short or low slope slide

a3 Little difficult

  • Swimming not exceeding 30 m in calm water
  • Progression in weak current
  • Easy jump from 3 to 5 m
  • Long or medium slope slide

a4 Somewhat difficult

  • Prolonged immersion resulting in a significant heat loss
  • Progression in medium current
  • Wide siphon of less than 1 m in length and/or depth
  • Easy jump from 5 to 8 m
  • Difficult jump less than 5 m
  • Large slide or steep slide

a5 Difficult

  • Prolonged immersion in cold water resulting in significant heat loss
  • Progression in a fairly strong current, which can disrupt swimming trajectories, bins, stops or resumptions
  • Obligatory difficulties linked to specific white water phenomena — whirlpools, drifting
  • Wide siphon up to 2 m in length and/or depth
  • Easy jump from 8 to 10 m
  • Difficult jump from 5 to 8 m

a6 Very difficult

  • Progression in strong current making swimming trajectories, bins, stops or resumptions difficult to execute
  • Marked water movements — drifting, backwash, waves, whirlpools
  • Wide siphon up to 3 m deep and/or long
  • Technical siphon up to a maximum of 1 m, with possible current or pathway

a7 Extremely difficult

  • Progression in a very strong current making swimming trajectories, bins, stops or resumptions extremely difficult
  • Violent water movements that can lead to a prolonged blockage of the canyoneer
  • Siphon more than 3 m long and/or deep
  • Technical and engaged siphon of more than 1 m with current or path, or without visibility

C — Commitment & scope

The third column has nothing to do with technique. It answers one question: how far in can you be, and how long does it take to get out?

I Quick exit

  • Possibility to get out of the flood easily and quickly
  • Easy escape routes present on all parts of the course
  • Total time — approach, descent, return — less than 2 hours

II 15 minutes

  • Possibility to get out of the flood within 15 minutes of progress at most
  • Escape within ½ hour of progress at most
  • Total time between 2 and 4 hours

III ½ hour

  • Possibility to get out of the flood within ½ hour of progress at most
  • Escape within 1 hour of progress at most
  • Total time between 4 and 8 hours

IV 1 hour

  • Possibility to get out of the flood within 1 hour of progress at most
  • Escape within 2 hours of progress at most
  • Total time between 8 hours and 1 day

V 2 hours

  • Possibility to get out of the flood within 2 hours at most
  • Escape within 4 hours of progress at most
  • Total time between 1 and 2 days

VI 2+ hours

  • Possibility to get out of the flood in more than 2 hours of progression
  • Escape requires more than 4 hours of progress
  • Total time greater than 2 days
